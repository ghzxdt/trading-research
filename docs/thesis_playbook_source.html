Thesis Playbook

How to read the trading-research terminal as a quant and day trader, where the information gaps are, and how we close them. Written 13 November 2026 against the app at commit 08c8e9a and data through the 12 November close.
What this is. A research process for the tickers the app is built around (SPY, QQQ, IWM and their leveraged ETFs). It describes how evidence is read and tested, not what to buy. The app never places orders, sizing stays blocked, and nothing here is personal investment advice. Every number is taken from the app's own data; where something has not been checked it says so.

3. Six routes to a thesis

Each route is a different question asked of the same tickers. Routes A and B have tested evidence today. C to F are where the information gaps are largest.

Route A. Oversold bounce over several days Evidence exists

The idea. After a sharp drop, SPY and QQQ have tended to recover over the next few sessions. This is the app's registered route and the only one that drives the headline.

The last column is the honest caution: since 2010, simply holding the ETF for the same share of time earned more per unit of drawdown in every row. The RSI(14) rows have about 24 trades per period, which is a small sample.

How I read it

  • Which rule fired, on which ETF, and is its next-open pricing a Lead? If only the close pricing is, there is no setup the next morning.
  • QQQ evidence is stronger than SPY's in every row. IWM has none.
  • Then the cautions: an event inside the holding window, earnings, an inverted VIX curve.

Gaps

  • The headline counts close-only evidence (SPY RSI(2) below 10 is a Lead only at the close; it fired 24 times last year).
  • No view of the overnight gap against the evidence: a large gap up before the open has already taken part of the bounce.
  • No tracker for a setup that is open.
  • No test of whether the rules do better or worse by regime.

Close them by: requiring a next-open Lead for the headline and the log; splitting the next-open evidence by size of overnight gap in the backtester; adding an "open setups" panel (days held, distance to the exit level); registering one trend-and-volatility filter as a new backtester version before looking at results.

Route B. The close-to-open move Evidence exists Not usable from the page yet

The idea. In every rule above, buying at the signal close beats buying at the next open: SPY Bollinger +0.54% vs +0.46% per trade, QQQ RSI(2) +0.60% vs +0.48%. Part of the bounce happens overnight. For SPY RSI(2) the whole rated edge is in the close entry (192 trades, 64% wins, +0.57%; "No edge" from the next open).

How I read it

  • The decision has to be made a few minutes before 4:00 PM, on a close that has not printed yet.
  • Last night's page already gives the levels: "fires at or below" for each rule. At 3:50 PM I compare the live price with them.

Gaps

  • The page is built after the close, so it reports the signal when the close entry is gone.
  • No test of how often a rule that looked fired at 3:50 PM was still fired at 4:00 PM.

Close them by: a "pre-close check" on the Daily thesis, filled by the 3:50 PM collection: each rule, its trigger level, the 3:50 price and the distance. Then, from the minute bars as they build up, measure the 3:50-to-close flip rate. This turns the close-only evidence into something that can honestly be acted on and logged before the fact.

Route C. Event and catalyst days Size only, no direction

The idea. CPI, jobs and Fed days, mega-cap earnings and single-stock shocks change how much the index moves. The app's own test found jobs-morning gaps about 1.2 times normal and no directional edge (p = 0.69). So this is a route about the size of the move, which is what options price.

How I read it

  • Events table: typical SPY move on that kind of day against a normal day, with the number of days behind it.
  • Earnings table: do Yahoo and Nasdaq agree on the date, and QQQ's median reaction.
  • An event inside a Route A holding window is a caution to weigh against the setup, not a signal by itself.

Gaps

  • No release values and no expectation, so "surprise" cannot be measured.
  • News arrives late: on 12 November a 9:12 AM headline about Nvidia was first seen by the app at 7:39 PM, because collection ran only in the evening.
  • No news or single-stock shock flag on the Daily thesis.
  • No intraday profile of event days (when the move happens).

Close them by: the jobs and macro data fix already on the roadmap (BLS API, first-release values from ALFRED); a headline collection in the 8:00 AM run; a "large mover among the top holdings" flag from the daily prices already saved; event-day intraday profiles once enough minute bars exist (12 CPI days a year, so this takes a year to say much).

Route D. Intraday: opening range, VWAP and levels Not enough data

The idea. Trade the day's own structure: a break of the first 15 minutes' range confirmed by VWAP, or a fade back to it. The app has one registered rule (orb-continuation-v1) and the charting for it.

How I read it today

  • As observation only. The rule shows 5 trades from a prototype that predates the liquidity gate.
  • Levels are hypotheses. The options strike concentrations are unsigned and are not dealer positioning.

Gaps

  • About 9 sessions of minute bars. Zero liquidity samples: no daytime run has ever happened.
  • A suspect level: SPY's "observed overnight low" shows $711.46 against a $759.79 previous close. That looks like a bad print and needs a filter (not yet checked).
  • No base rates by time of day, and no link from the daily regime to intraday behaviour.
  • Paper observations are logged but outcomes are not resolved automatically.

Close them by: making the daytime runs happen (laptop awake or wake timers); a bad-tick filter on extended-hours levels; a descriptive page once 60 sessions exist (gap-fill rate, share of the day's range made in the first hour, how often the opening-range break holds), split by the daily regime; automatic outcome resolution for the ORB rule, which is fully determined by the bars.

Route E. Same-day options (0DTE) Not built

The idea. Express a view on today's move, its direction or its size, with options that expire at today's close. Section 4 sets out how I would add it.

Route F. Expression and leverage Partial

The idea. TQQQ, SQQQ and UPRO are ways to hold the same index view with more exposure; they scale gains and losses alike and lose ground in choppy markets.

How I read it

  • The risk card: the index move that wipes out the account at each leverage, a typical day, the worst day and the worst 5 sessions.

Gaps

  • The evidence is never shown in the instrument that would be traded. A +0.80% QQQ trade is not simply +2.4% in TQQQ over a 10-session hold.
  • No risk limit has been set, so no "allowed size" can be shown.

Close them by: pricing the same historical trades in TQQQ and UPRO in the backtester (their history starts in 2010, so there is no 1993-2009 holdout and the page must say so); one decision from you on a maximum loss per trade, which unlocks the size view.

4. Adding 0DTE research for these tickers

What can be traded same-day

  • SPY and QQQ: an expiry every trading day.
  • IWM: I believe it also has daily expiries; to be confirmed from the collected chains.
  • TQQQ, SQQQ, UPRO, SOXL and the rest, NVDL, TSLL: weekly expiries, so same-day only on Fridays; to be confirmed the same way.

The property that makes this researchable for free

A 0DTE position held to the close settles at a value fixed by the closing price alone. So the outcome needs only data the app already has. Only the entry price needs an option quote, taken at a fixed time. Forward paper scoring is therefore possible for hold-to-close structures. Anything that exits early needs intraday option quotes we do not have.

What free data cannot do

  • No history of 0DTE option prices before we start collecting. A true backtest of option profit and loss needs paid data.
  • Free quotes are delayed and indicative. Results must be shown at mid-price and at the worst case (buy at the ask, sell at the bid).
  • The current collector takes chains at 3:50 PM, ten minutes before expiry. That is too late to be useful here.

Guardrails

  • ETF options are American-style and settle in shares, and holders can exercise after the close. Short options can be assigned on an after-hours move, so research covers bought options and defined-risk spreads only.
  • A separate registered study with its own frozen version. It never feeds the daily headline.

6. How an idea becomes an edge here

  1. Write it down first. The rule, the entry time, the exit and the cost, before looking at results.
  2. Backtest against three hurdles: random entries in the same periods, buy-and-hold at the same exposure, and the untouched 1993-2009 holdout.
  3. Log it forward. Recorded before the entry, never edited, scored automatically.
  4. Review on a schedule, with the sample size in view. Change a rule only as a new version.
  5. Size last, once margin and risk limits are set and forward evidence exists.

7. The two apps: keep both, tighten the join

Research app: the desk

Collects the data, shows the thesis every evening and morning, and keeps the forward record. It has to be stable: its experiments are frozen and its logs cannot be edited.

Backtester: the lab

Tests ideas on the desk's saved prices: 90 indicator tests and a trade simulator so far, each written down before it runs. It is small (under 1,000 lines) and meant to change often.

How they connect today. The lab reads the desk's saved daily prices and uses its Python. It writes one evidence file. The desk reads that file, recomputes the same rules itself, and checks that the dates each rule fired agree.

What worksWhat is weak
  • A clean hand-off: one file, read-only in each direction, so a lab change cannot break the desk's records.
  • The same discipline on both sides: registered before testing, a 1993-2009 holdout, append-only records.
  • Reproducible: the lab's 90 results were identical when re-run on the new laptop.
  • Honest verdicts: a "Lead" must beat random entries in both periods, and the report says where buy-and-hold was better.
  • The evidence refreshes only when you click "Run trade simulation". The current file is from 9 November and checks dates through 6 November.
  • The desk does not notice an old or damaged file (gaps 1 and 2).
  • Each rule is written twice, once per app. The date check guards this, and only this.
  • "Backtests" live in three places: the desk's Backtests tab (four frozen older rules), the lab, and the intraday rule registry.
  • The lab reads daily prices only, so it cannot yet test anything intraday. Its 24 Leads come from 48 related results with no correction for testing many at once.

My recommendation

Keep both, and do not merge the code. The separation is what lets the lab change freely while the desk's forward record stays trustworthy. Merging would make every experiment a change to the app that holds the frozen logs. What they need is to behave as one product:

  1. Run the simulation automatically each week after an evening refresh.
  2. Give the evidence file a checked format, and have the desk show its age and refuse a damaged one.
  3. Link the Daily thesis evidence table to the lab's full report.
  4. Send all new testing to the lab; leave the desk's Backtests tab as the frozen record of the first four rules.
  5. Teach the lab to read the minute bars once 60 sessions exist.

Is it worth working on both?

Yes. They answer different questions, and the edge-finding work is mostly in the lab:
